Two diagrams. The first answers the brief's three §1 questions directly, in place, on the diagram: data flow, which Sand products are used and why, what is built custom and why, build vs. buy per component. Not used, D2

Health Atlas

Geographic mapping needs real facility coordinates. The sample's gps_lat/gps_lon are uniform random within Rwanda's bounding box (verified: every province spans the full extent), so facility-point mapping is impossible this cycle. District-grain choropleth is the honest substitute; a full Atlas integration is a D3/D4 item once real coordinates exist.

Not used, D2

Health Outcome Tracker

The bulletin's facility-to-district-to-national roll-up is real, but it's built directly in the Gold layer (district_quarter, nmr_district_quarter) rather than on this product. No working, Rwanda-configured instance is established to plug a new bulletin into (evidence: a pillar name on an AWS Marketplace listing plus one employee's LinkedIn profile), so the roll-up logic stays bespoke Hamilton nodes instead of migrating to product configuration.

Not used, D2

Health Insight Engine

Documented shape: AI-driven anomaly detection and alerting. The closest live analog, Rwanda's National Health Intelligence Center, already runs outbreak surveillance, but the brief's four required metrics (top facilities, maternal indicators, performance scores, trend) are descriptive analytics, not alerting: no fit even where a confirmed instance exists. The two checks built instead are simple, seeded, and auditable, the opposite of an opaque model: a DHO needs a traceable method, not a score to trust unread.

Buy, scoped

Analytics Template Toolkit

Apache Superset, the toolkit's own reporting layer. Adopted for the server-rendered dashboard surface at the connected central MoH office only, where power and connectivity are reliable. Not used for the bulletin or email edition, and distinct from the browser-native Explore surface (Mosaic over DuckDB-WASM), which is scoped in this design but not built this cycle: Superset needs a live connection that surface doesn't, and can't substitute for it in the offline majority of districts.

Not used, D2

HealthOS Data Models

This binds directly to DHIS2's own public data model (org_unit, period, data_element): the naming conflict resolved here (Bluelake's UI vs. the CSVs' fields vs. DHIS2's UIDs vs. the brief's prose) is specific to this engagement's sources. A generic product can't pre-resolve a conflict defined by which systems a Ministry runs and what its analysts call things. The crosswalk and silver layer are built from scratch instead, deliberately shaped to match what the product likely targets: DHIS2's own grain, ICD-10 perinatal codes, WHO GHO indicator definitions. Weakest evidence grade of the five: the product's likely architecture is inference from generic DHIS2/openHIE/medallion patterns, not anything Sand-specific.

2Data-mart ERD

Not a normalized relational schema. There is no web of pairwise foreign-key joins here. The real shape is six heterogeneous source tables, each with its own key and grain, unpivoted through one crosswalk mapping into a single canonical long-format fact table. Drawing this as a conventional star or snowflake ERD would misrepresent the architecture, so it is drawn as what it actually is: sources feeding a mapping mechanism feeding one hub.
