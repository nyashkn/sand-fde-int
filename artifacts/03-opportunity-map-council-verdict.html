Opportunity map & council verdict

Problems A, B and C in the brief are stated as solutions. This maps them back to the underlying needs, then puts the selection through a six-seat adversarial council — three rounds, anonymised cross-examination, confidence-weighted tally. The council surfaced a fourth opportunity the brief never names, and killed two of its own members' proposals on the way to a verdict.

O4 is the finding

Two seats reached it independently, by different methods. It matters because the Director's actual sentence was "our data is a mess, we cannot make good decisions" — a trust complaint, not a throughput complaint. A, B and C all answer throughput. None of them answers O4 directly.

"The Director bought relief from not trusting the numbers she's shown, not a bulletin." — Socrates, Round 1
"Maybe none of A/B/C is right — the true need is a Director who can't trust any number she's shown, not a missing report or dashboard." — Rams, restate gate

The practical consequence: whichever solution ships must carry S-D1 and S-D2 as requirements, not nice-to-haves. That is why the recommended outcome statement below includes end-to-end figure traceability — it is the part that addresses the need actually voiced.

2 · A, B and C as competing solutions

Compared on the only axis that matters for a 6-week first engagement: can it be finished, proven, and still running after the FDE leaves the country.

Solution ★ Selected
A

Automate the bulletin

Addresses O1. Partially addresses O4 if traceability is built in.

Whole delivery path already exists: DHIS2 pull → conformed mart → Superset template → scheduled report. Nothing greenfield. Has a measurable baseline (40 hrs) that exists today, so the win is provable inside the sprint.

Weakness: automates the mess rather than resolving it. Touches none of the 175 paper-only facilities.
Solution
B

Real-time facility status

Addresses O2. This is the Solutions Manager's pre-committed narrative.

175 of ~250 facilities have no digital capture at all. There is no sensor, no app, no feed. "Real-time" for 70% of the fleet is not a hard build — it is a UI over a null field, plus a second unstarted project to digitise paper.

Weakness: its honest scope is "real-time for 30% of facilities" — a materially smaller promise than the one being asked for.
Solution
C

Unified TB/HIV patient view

Addresses O3. Highest clinical value of the three.

Probabilistic identity matching across CommCare silos with no shared identifier, in six weeks, with no MoH clinical-safety review process to catch a bad merge.

Weakness: the failure mode is a false patient-record merge — wrong regimen, missed contraindication. A coroner's inquest, not a bug ticket.

Note S-C2 in the tree: a weekly reconciliation report flags co-infected patients for a human to review, without merging any records. It captures much of O3's value with none of C's tail risk — and the brief never considers it. That is the practical payoff of separating opportunities from solutions.

3 · What the council actually did

The interesting result is not the answer. It is that the first answer was wrong in a way that looked exactly like agreement.

Round 1 — apparent unanimity

All six seats independently backed Problem A. On a single-provider council that is a warning sign, not a result — six runs of one model landing on the same answer is the failure mode a multi-provider panel exists to catch.

Round 2 — the unanimity breaks

Cross-examination established that the six seats had converged on a label while meaning four incompatible things: ship the minimum wiring; restructure the sprint so the Ministry's Digital Health Officer co-builds the pipeline; add a read-only situation-map as a hedge; quietly instrument toward B for political cover. These cannot coexist in six weeks with two engineers.

Two seats then killed their own proposals

"The unanimity is not independent convergence — five of six converged on 'A' while smuggling in incompatible payloads. That's anchoring on a label, not agreement on a thing." — Socrates, Round 2
"Commit to the handover-act, conditioned explicitly on a named person — absent that it reverts to bare A, and I will not pretend that is still capacity transfer. Secondary preference: none — the co-build is dead." — Meadows (1.5× domain-weight seat), Round 3 — abandoning her own signature proposal
"Via negativa: remove the exposure whose safety depends on someone else's future restraint. I fold the barbell." — Taleb, Round 3 — withdrawing his own hedge after Machiavelli showed a "read-only" label cannot bind how others re-use an artifact

The heaviest-weighted seat conceding its own position is the strongest available evidence the final convergence is reasoned rather than conformist. It is not proof.

5 · What the council does not know

Leading with the gaps, not the conclusion.

  • Nobody has asked whether a named DHO has cleared hours. Three full rounds argued about how much capacity transfer Problem A requires — and the entire debate turned on an empirical, checkable fact that a single email would answer in a day. The council resolved it by argument instead of by asking. This is a finding about the deliberation, not just the problem.
  • Whether the 40 hours/month is measured or self-reported. Every "high ROI, low risk" argument for A silently assumes it is accurate.
  • Whether the Solutions Manager's pre-discovery B narrative is backed by a real external commitment — a funder deliverable or NHIC mandate — that would make choosing A organisationally costly regardless of technical merit.
  • Whether the Director herself, not just the Solutions Manager, has been told B and C are deferred rather than rejected, and agrees to that sequencing.
  • Whether DHIS2's aggregate figures are ever verified against facility source records, or whether the bulletin has been faithfully automating a noisy input for years.
  • Whether machine-readable historical bulletins even exist to validate a new pipeline against — which would make kill-criterion K2 below unachievable as written.

6 · Kill criteria

Observable without re-convening anything, tied to a threshold and a date. If one trips, the verdict is invalid — not "worth revisiting".

  1. If by end of Week 1 no MoH IT lead has named a specific Digital Health Officer with confirmed cleared hours, the handover act has no subject. Formally downgrade the plan to A-minimal and stop describing the deliverable as capacity transfer — per the domain seat's own condition.
  2. If by end of Week 3 the pipeline's output for two already-published historical quarters deviates >5% on any headline indicator from the manually-compiled bulletin, the "this is wiring, not R&D" premise is falsified. Replan weeks 4–6 around reconciliation, not handover polish.
  3. If at any point the Solutions Manager or Country Director represents real-time facility status or Health Atlas as delivered or in progress externally, that is the political-capture failure flagged by the dealbreaker vote. Correct the record with the Country Director within 48 hours; if uncorrected, escalate for explicit sequencing with MoH leadership.
  4. If direct observation of the analyst in Weeks 1–2 — stopwatch, not self-report — puts real compilation time outside a 25–55 hr/month band, recalculate the ROI case before Week 3. The ranking against B and C can change.
  5. If Week 1–2 technical discovery finds DHIS2 export schemas or indicator definitions vary materially across the 45 HealthTrack hospitals and 30 OpenMRS clinics, "nothing on A's path is greenfield" is false for a real fraction of the fleet. Scope down to fewer facilities with explicit gap flags, or renegotiate the schedule.

7 · Verdict

Commit to A-plus-handover-act

Problem A — bulletin automation — plus exactly one bounded capacity-transfer act: a named Digital Health Officer personally runs the pipeline restart once, unassisted, while the FDE watches, before exit. No joint architecture. No co-build. No hedged B artifact.

  1. Ship A — DHIS2 pull → conformed mart → Superset template → scheduled report. Every figure traceable to a source record (this is the part that answers O4).
  2. One handover act — the named DHO runs the restart themselves, once, before exit. Hours, not a redesign.
  3. Condition it in Week 1 — ask for the named person immediately. If refused, downgrade honestly to A-minimal rather than letting the capacity-transfer claim survive by default.
  4. Defer B and C on the record — in writing, to the Director, as deferred not rejected. Not quietly.
  5. Ship no B-shaped artifact — not even read-only. A status map's visual grammar asserts a freshness a "stale" label cannot revoke, and read-only does not bind how others re-use it.

Concrete next step: send a written request to the MoH IT lead by end of Week 1, Day 2, naming a specific Digital Health Officer and asking them to confirm cleared hours for a Week 6 handover-and-restart session.

Honest limits of this verdict

Convergence risk: medium-high. This was a single-provider council by mandate. Six personas of one model are not six independent opinions, and no seat at any round seriously entertained B or C as the final pick. It is not possible from this transcript to separate "A is genuinely correct" from "this model's priors, run six ways, reliably land on A."

Zero empirical evidence. Across three rounds, no seat produced a measurement. Every argument reasons from the brief. That is precisely why Section 6 is written as falsifiable field checks rather than conclusions.

The counter-evidence is real but bounded: Round 2 dismantled the false unanimity, and two seats abandoned their own proposals under pressure. That is a higher bar than a council restating Round 1 with more confidence — and still not a substitute for a second model family.

Council composition, method, and protocol deviations

Panel (6 seats, method-diverse by construction — no two share a reasoning method):

  • Socrates — elenchic questioning. Assumption destruction.
  • Meadows — causal-loop mapping. Domain-weight seat, 1.5×, on the grounds that opportunity mapping is leverage-point location. Locked before any analysis.
  • Machiavelli — incentive backward-induction. Power and revealed preference.
  • Torvalds — empirical reduction to practice. Ship it and maintain it.
  • Rams — subtractive essentialism. The user decides; design honesty.
  • Taleb — tail stress-testing. Fragility and skin in the game.

Protocol: restate gate → Round 1 independent (300w) → Round 2 anonymised cross-examination with anti-conformity directive (250w) → Round 3 crystallisation with structured stance lines (100w) → confidence-weighted tally → separate Chairman synthesis.

Deviations, recorded rather than smoothed over:

  • Single provider. Claude-only by explicit user mandate. Provider spread scores 1/5. This is the weakest link in the diversity design and is not minimised anywhere in this document.
  • No native council subagent types in this harness, so each seat ran as a generic subagent with its persona identity block inlined verbatim. That is a prompting workaround, not a model-level guarantee.
  • Meadows and Torvalds are a genuine polarity pair forced onto the same provider by the Claude-only mandate. Their Round 2 clash is well-argued but carries no architectural independence.
  • Three seats returned compressed stubs; their full text was recovered from their own transcripts rather than re-prompted. The recovered text is presented above as delivered.
  • Chairman was dispatched as a separate agent rather than synthesised by the coordinator, specifically because the coordinator had already argued for Problem A earlier in the session and would have been auditing its own prior. The Chairman still shares a provider with the panel.
  • One Chairman error corrected here: the verdict text stated "no restate gate triggered". The restate gate did run, and produced the highest-signal output of the session — all six seats independently rejected the A/B/C framing before seeing each other's work.
