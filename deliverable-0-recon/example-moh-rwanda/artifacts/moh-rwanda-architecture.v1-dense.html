Rwanda MoH — systems map, persona journeys & data-protection posture

A single MECE model of the Rwandan Ministry of Health digital-health stack: systems × actors × edges × controls. Nine system categories (A reporting · B facility/community EMR · C community mHealth · D supply chain · E laboratory · F identity/registries/interop · G workforce/financing/admin · H intelligence · I external/partner), six personas, thirty-two traced edges, and a control-by-control reading against Law n° 058/2021. Gaps the research could not verify are drawn dashed — never papered over.

In production (documented) Interop backbone / live feedback loop Planned · under development · unverified (dashed, ⚠-tagged) Defunct (Babyl, Aug 2023)

1 · Executive summary

  • National-first, government-hosted stack. The National Data Centre has hosted DHIS2/RHMIS and the RHIE since 2013[2.5]; every health facility runs its own on-prem OpenMRS/eBuzima server (42 hospitals + 450+ health centres, ~60% of facilities)[1.1]; the community tier is mobile-first and offline-first, scaling to 58,567 CHWs by end-2026[1.8]. No verified migration of the national stack to AWS/Azure/GCP as of 2024–26.
  • The OpenHIE interoperability backbone is real and live. OpenHIM (v8.2, Grafana/Prometheus) mediates a Client Registry (UPID keyed to NIDA, auto-filling ~90% of registration fields)[2.1], Facility/Provider/Lab registries, and a FHIR Shared Health Record; the EMR↔VLSM FHIR lab flow is implemented and validated at 197 PEPFAR sites across 30 districts[4.1].
  • Two feedback loops make it a “live” HIS, not a passive reporter: CHW d-IDS alerts → eIDSR (P5 → P2), and facility EMR → DHIS2 monthly aggregate → NHIC dashboards (P3 → P1). The NHIC — a six-layer data lakehouse launched 3 Apr 2025[2.3][2.4] — is the national AI/analytics layer.
  • AI is moving to the edge. d-IDS decision support inside the national cEMR (24.2% fewer referrals)[3.6], mUbuzima ML wound-image screening offline on CHW phones[3.7], Gates × OpenAI Horizon1000 (up to $50M, 1,000 clinics by 2028, Rwanda first)[1.2], an Anthropic–GoR MOU[5.12]. Babyl — the one documented PHI-out-of-Rwanda service (Babylon’s AWS UK, ~2.8M users) — wound down in Aug 2023 with no successor[3.8].
  • Data protection is strong where it is documented, thin where it is not. Identity control, RBAC, in-country residency, and the IeDEA C# de-identification pipeline (world-class for research reuse)[5.8] are evidenced; the Law 058/2021 close-list[5.1][5.2] is consent capture, at-rest encryption, production audit trails, DPO appointment, and NCSA registration — the gates to clear before Horizon1000-class AI pipelines go live.

P1
National policy / planning analyst — NHIC user
primary: H1 NHIC portal (nhic.moh.gov.rw) · secondary: A1 · A2 · B1 (aggregates) · G2 · G7 · G10
  1. Morning brief. Opens NHIC executive dashboards — maternal & child trends, epidemic signals. H1
  2. Trace a signal. District malaria-admission spike → drill into NHIC’s 12 source streams. H1
  3. Cross-check lineage. Corroborate against eIDSR Tracker + HIV CBS built with HISP Rwanda. A2 A3
  4. Overlay HR + supply. HWMS (4×4 reform staffing) + eLMIS commodity indicators. G2 D1
  5. Draft policy brief. Country-driven HIS evidence, per WHO’s Rwanda case study. H1
  6. Feed planning. Brief → Health Sector Strategic Plan adjustments + NHIC roadmap. H1
DATA — consumes: 12 NHIC source streams (CHW → referral hospital, surveillance, CRVS, workforce, finance) · produces: ministerial dashboards, AI-assisted forecasts, briefings.
P2
Epi / surveillance officer — RBC / district (IDSR)
primary: A2 DHIS2 Tracker eIDSR (cbs2.moh.gov.rw/idsr) · secondary: A3 · H1 · alerts in from C2/C3
  1. Receive alert. Facility clinician or CHW flags a suspected notifiable condition. A2
  2. Enter case. DHIS2 Tracker case record; individual follow-up, case-based surveillance. A2
  3. Link lab. Specimens + results linked to the case; HIV CBS functional at 166 sites (CDC). A3 E2
  4. Threshold logic. DHIS2 program rules trigger outbreak alerts. A2
  5. Bulletin. Weekly IDSR aggregates roll up nationally and feed NHIC. A1 H1
  6. Pandemic mode. Modules adapted for case management + contact tracing (COVID pattern). A4
DATA — consumes: suspected-case notifications from facilities + CHWs · produces: case line lists, weekly epi bulletins, outbreak alerts.
P3
Hospital clinician — OpenMRS eBuzima
primary: B1 OpenMRS + F6 SHR + F3 UPID lookup · secondary: A1 (monthly upload) · G11 (eligibility)
  1. Patient arrives. Search SHR via Client Registry; NIDA auto-fills ~90% of registration. F3 F6
  2. Encounter. Chart on the facility’s on-prem OpenMRS server; ART/TB regimens, labs, billing. B1
  3. Cross-facility lookup. Pull prior diagnoses + medications from other facilities via SHR. F6
  4. Lab order. B1 → OpenHIM → FHIR ServiceRequest in HAPI store → VLSM; results return by FHIR query. B1 F2 E2
  5. Monthly upload. Aggregate indicators pushed to RHMIS via the Monthly Report Tool. A1
  6. Research extract. IeDEA de-identification (C# identifier-stripping) before any US upload. B1
DATA — produces: encounter-level clinical records, monthly HMIS aggregates, de-identified research extracts · consumes: SHR priors, lab results, ART regimens.
P4
District Health Officer — DHO + PBF verifier
primary: A1 DHIS2 district dashboards + G10 PBF dashboard (aggregate.moh.gov.rw/pbfrwanda) · secondary: G2 · G11 · SISCOM
  1. District dashboard. Monthly ANC, deliveries, immunisation, OPD indicators. A1
  2. PBF verification. Facility quantity + quality claims cross-checked against HMIS. G10
  3. CHW performance. SISCOM cooperative reports via supervising health centres. C1
  4. Workforce. Verify 4×4 staffing-rule deployment (4 GPs + 4 nurse anaesthetists) in HWMS. G2
  5. Imihigo. District performance rolls into national performance contracts. G10
  6. Feed NHIC. Cleaned HMIS stream consumed by national dashboards. H1
DATA — consumes: HMIS aggregates, PBF claims, CHW cooperative reports · produces: district performance reviews, PBF verification.
P5
Community Health Worker — CHW
primary: B2/C1 cEMR · eCHIS + d-IDS · (legacy C2 RapidSMS) · secondary: A2 alerts out · A1 via SISCOM
  1. Work list. cEMR/eCHIS app shows assigned households (>45,000 active CHWs). B2 C1
  2. Household visit. Offline-first Android: register pregnancies, ANC, malnutrition + NCD screens. B2
  3. AI screening. mUbuzima ML on incision photos predicts surgical-site infection — offline. C1 I13
  4. d-IDS guidance. Case registration → symptom assessment → treat/refer (−24.2% referrals). B2
  5. Treat or refer. iCCM at the household, or app-prompted referral to the health centre. B2
  6. SISCOM report. Monthly indicators to supervising health centre → compiled into DHIS2. A1
  7. Surveillance alert. Notifiable suspicion pushed to eIDSR — closes the loop to P2. A2
DATA — produces: household-level records, SISCOM aggregates, d-IDS alerts · consumes: work lists, app content, training (C7 AKIISA).
P6
Citizen / patient
primary: G11 RSSB Mutuelle de Santé · formerly I12 Babyl · touched by: B1 / A1 at point of service
  1. Enrol. Mutuelle de Santé via the cell coordinator; 93% of the insured were in CBHI (2019). G11
  2. Membership proof. Paper card + digitised record keyed to national ID. G11 F3
  3. Visit. Eligibility verified at intake; encounter in OpenMRS or HMIS aggregate. B1 A1
  4. Teleconsult. Babyl served ~2.8M users until the Aug 2023 shutdown; no successor. I12
  5. Billing. Claim against CBHI/RSSB — manual; no automated EMR↔insurance link documented. G11
  6. Upward flow. Anonymised encounters → monthly DHIS2 → NHIC dashboards. A1 H1
  7. Future. Health Portal will let citizens view records pulled from B1 + B2. B4
DATA — produces: enrolment records, premium payments, encounter-level utilisation · consumes: membership verification, teleconsultation advice.

4 · Integration & data-flow — the OpenHIM bus

The two clay edges are what make this a live HIS: CHW d-IDS → eIDSR (community → surveillance) and the EMR → OpenHIM → DHIS2 aggregate → NHIC chain (facility → national analytics). Everything crossing the bus inherits its consent model: record/element-level deny at the patient’s request[4.2]. Backbone evidence: RHIE report §5.3.2/§6.3[4.1] · OpenHIE impact story[4.2].

Law n° 058/2021 (in force 15 Oct 2021[5.1][5.2]; NCSA/DPO Rwanda as regulator[5.5]; 48-hour breach notification[5.6]; storage-in-Rwanda default under art. 50[5.4]). Each obligation is mapped to what the MoH stack demonstrably does today and to the benchmark pattern from Sand-class health-data platforms (Sand, Helium Health, Zipline — all three converge on the GDPR processor pattern)[5.10][5.11].

The close-list before the next pipeline goes live

1 · Consent capture. Encode a per-system, patient-facing consent workflow (the RHIE deny model already proves the exchange layer can honour it) — otherwise routine-care processing leans entirely on public-health grounds.

2 · At-rest encryption. Document disk/DB-level encryption for national production servers (NDC + facility OpenMRS); art. 11 names it as a sensitive-data safeguard.

3 · Production audit architecture. Immutable, in-country, who-read-which-record logs — NCSA inspections should not require cross-border evidence requests.

4 · DPO + NCSA registration. Appoint a DPO and backfill controller/processor registration for MoH and named vendors; the Oct-2023 transition window has closed but inspections and fines remain available.

5 · Cross-border certificates. Any PHI outside Rwanda (incl. the planned cloud OpenMRS instances and any AI-vendor pipeline) needs an art. 49 certificate + DPIA + stated art. 5 ground; African regions (AWS af-south-1, Azure SA-North) simplify the application. Encode heir designation in the B4 Health Portal and a hard human-in-the-loop rule on AI-assisted triage/risk-scoring.
