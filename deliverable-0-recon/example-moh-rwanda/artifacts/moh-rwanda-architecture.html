Ministry of Health · Rwanda · digital-health landscape — architecture briefing

Rwanda's national digital-health stack, at a glance

A national-first, government-hosted, OpenHIE-interoperable stack: 24 systems across 9 categories, hosted at the National Data Centre and in Kigali. It is strong on identity (UPID/NIDA), aggregate reporting (DHIS2 as the national HIS since 2012), supply chain (e-LMIS), lab integration (EMR↔VLSM over OpenHIM + HAPI FHIR), and analytics (NHIC, launched Apr 2025)2 — and it is pushing AI to the edge (d-IDS in cEMR, mUbuzima ML on CHW phones). The open work sits in the data-protection layer: consent capture, at-rest encryption, production audit and DPO/NCSA registration under Law No. 058/2021.

06Data-protection posture vs Law No. 058/2021

Each obligation from Rwanda's data-protection law against what the deployed MoH stack demonstrably does today. The stack protects data mainly through access control, residency and infrastructure — the open work is consent capture, at-rest encryption, production audit and governance roles.3

Obligation (Law 058/2021) Status How the MoH stack addresses it today
Access control (RBAC)security TOMs Documented OpenMRS role/privilege model + DHIS2 user roles/sharing; F5 Provider Registry gates EMR access by licence validity; NIDA-linked identity via F3 Client Registry.
Data residencyart. 49 / 50 Documented DHIS2 + RHIE hosted at the National Data Centre since 2013; storage-in-Rwanda default. IeDEA US transfers occur only with government + ethics permission (lawful pathway).
De-identificationart. 11 safeguards Documented Strongest documented control: IeDEA OpenMRS C# identifier-stripping before research upload8. Scope is research reuse, not routine clinical care.
Consent captureart. 5 / 6 Partial RHIE implements a record- and element-level deny model at the patient's request; NHIC claims anonymization at the presentation layer. No public per-system capture workflow for routine care.
Encryptionart. 11 · transit / rest Partial SSL/TLS documented in transit (OpenMRS Rwanda, OpenHIE). At-rest encryption for national production servers is not publicly documented.
Audit loggingart. 47 Partial OpenHIM dashboard gives a transaction audit log (Grafana/Prometheus); DHIS2 audit API exists. No public production audit-trail spec (who-read-which-record, immutability, retention).
Breach notificationart. 41 · 48h to NCSA Partial NCSA breach-notification form is ready with a Health sector tick-box; no public MoH runbook or documented 48-hour notification case.
DPIA for high-riskart. 39-equiv. Gap No public DPIA register for national systems; AI-on-health pipelines (NHIC, Horizon1000, Insightiv) have not disclosed DPIA outputs.
DPO appointmentart. 37-equiv. Gap MoH is controller of record; no public DPO appointment disclosed. Interim DPO can sit in the MoH CIO office.
Automated-decisioning guard-railart. 22-equiv. Gap d-IDS, NHIC AI, Horizon1000, Anthropic MOU — none documented with a formal human-in-the-loop override. Flagged for the Anthropic MOU by Tech Policy Press12.
Heir designationart. 26-equiv. Gap Not implemented in any documented Rwanda system; the B4 Health Portal (under development) is the natural place to encode it.

The one-line read

Rwanda runs a national-first, OpenHIE-interoperable stack that is genuinely strong on identity, aggregate reporting, supply chain, lab integration and analytics — and its IeDEA de-identification control is world-class for research reuse. The gap that matters before any new health-data pipeline goes live is the practice layer of Law 058/2021: consent capture, at-rest encryption, production audit, DPO appointment and NCSA registration.
