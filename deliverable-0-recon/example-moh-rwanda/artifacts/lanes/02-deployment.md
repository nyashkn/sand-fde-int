## Overview

Rwanda's health stack runs on a **national-first, government-hosted model with facility and community edge tiers**: the Ministry of Health (with RBC and, since 2025, the NHIC) centralizes national systems in the **National Data Center (NDC)**, which "opened its doors to the Ministry of Health in 2013 – and most web-enabled systems are now hosted on the world-class infrastructure" ([Rwanda Digital Health Strategic Plan 2018–2023, p. 20](https://extranet.who.int/countryplanningcycles/sites/default/files/public_file_rep/RWA_Rwanda_Digital-Health-Strategy_2018-2023.Pdf)). The historical HISP-Rwanda project pattern was a transition from "**hosted on HISP servers**" toward "**hosted on the RBC/MOH server**" (HISP Rwanda project documentation, via Perplexity extraction — URL not in dump; treat as medium confidence). Facility care runs on **on-premises servers at district hospitals** (per the 2018 strategy: "nearly all district hospitals now are connected to the national fiber optic internet connections"; "local servers for hosting the EMR"), while the community tier is **mobile-first** (SMS/feature phones, migrating to smartphones and offline-capable Android apps). The **NHIC (launched Apr 2025)** adds a centralized analytics layer: an automated ETL pipeline "replicated across secure, redundant systems" feeding a hot/cold-tiered data warehouse and Apache Superset dashboards ([NHIC frontend architecture text](https://nhic.moh.gov.rw/)). The clear open questions are *current* production hosting of DHIS2/eLMIS and NHIC's physical hosting — public sources describe intent and architecture but not today's colocation contracts.

## Discovered inventory — where each system runs

**National tier (NDC / government data centre, Kigali)**
- **HMIS / RHMIS on DHIS2** — web-based, countrywide since Feb 2012, collects from 700+ public facilities; DHIS2 instances hosted at the **National Data Center** at **~$5,000–7,000/month** (noted as high vs international cloud in 2018). Sources: [docs.dhis2.org — Rwanda HMIS user story](https://docs.dhis2.org/en/topics/user-stories/rwanda-hmis-powered-by-dhis2.html), [WHO extranet strategy PDF (pp. 20, 40, 58)](https://extranet.who.int/countryplanningcycles/sites/default/files/public_file_rep/RWA_Rwanda_Digital-Health-Strategy_2018-2023.Pdf)
- **eIDSR (disease surveillance)** — on the DHIS2 platform since 2014 (migrated from TracNet); real-time reporting from facilities into a central database. Source: [WHO extranet strategy PDF (p. 45)](https://extranet.who.int/countryplanningcycles/sites/default/files/public_file_rep/RWA_Rwanda_Digital-Health-Strategy_2018-2023.Pdf)
- **COVID-19 vaccination & testing** — DHIS2 modules used "paperless"; testing run on **DHIS2 Android Capture App on tablets/mobile** at facilities. Sources: [dhis2.org — COVID vaccination](https://dhis2.org/rwanda-covid-vaccination/), [dhis2.org — COVID testing](https://dhis2.org/rwanda-covid-testing/)
- **RHIE / RHEA (interoperability layer)** — central infrastructure "is still in place and is located in the **National Data Center**"; components = Shared Health Record (OpenMRS-based), Terminology Service, Client Registry (Ubudehe-based), Provider/Facility Registries, and an OpenHIM interoperability layer; Rwamagana district pilot (maternal health) no longer functional as of 2018. Sources: [WHO extranet strategy PDF (pp. 30–32)](https://extranet.who.int/countryplanningcycles/sites/default/files/public_file_rep/RWA_Rwanda_Digital-Health-Strategy_2018-2023.Pdf)
- **Health Data Warehouse** — national aggregation layer for the multiple DHIS2 instances, feeding dashboards and (per plan) the Citizen's Health Portal. Source: [WHO extranet strategy PDF (pp. 40–42)](https://extranet.who.int/countryplanningcycles/sites/default/files/public_file_rep/RWA_Rwanda_Digital-Health-Strategy_2018-2023.Pdf)
- **ePROGESA (CNTS blood bank)** — proprietary (Mak-System, France), runs **on a central server**, used at central level + 4 regional blood-bank centres; ~$30k/yr licence. Source: [WHO extranet strategy PDF (pp. 48–49)](https://extranet.who.int/countryplanningcycles/sites/default/files/public_file_rep/RWA_Rwanda_Digital-Health-Strategy_2018-2023.Pdf)
- **NHIC (National Health Intelligence Center, Apr 2025)** — centralized six-layer data pipeline: sources → automated ETL → replication across "secure, redundant systems" → centralized data warehouse (hot/cold tiering) → Superset dashboards, open APIs (`api.nhic.moh.gov.rw`), downloadable datasets; portal terms governed by Rwandan law; **physical HQ at the RURA Building, Kiyovu (Péage), Nyarugenge District, Kigali** ([Perplexity NHIC-host pass](out/.raw/pplx_deploy_7_nhic_host.json)); frontend hosted on a **kwikkoders.com subdomain** (branded "NHIC — National Health Intelligence Center | Ministry of Health"), which indicates kwikkoders as platform builder/operator (role: medium confidence); TBI (Tony Blair Institute) provided technical expertise for the data hub. Sources: [NHIC frontend architecture text](https://nhic.moh.gov.rw/), [TBI / Institute for Global Change](https://institute.global/insights/public-services/how-rwanda-is-using-data-to-deliver-better-health-care), [MoH news 03 Apr 2025](https://www.moh.gov.rw/news-detail/new-health-intelligence-center-to-drive-real-time-evidence-based-decisions)

**Facility tier (on-prem, district/referral hospitals + health centres)**
- **OpenMRS-based EMR (cEMR/eBuzima lineage)** — deployed on **servers at every health facility** ("the second option, currently in use, is the installation of the EMR system on servers at every health facility"); HIV module in 362 health facilities, billing in 8 district hospitals (2018); the plan was a mixed model (central server for some hospitals, local for the rest) migrating to centralized hosting. Source: [WHO extranet strategy PDF (pp. 33–34)](https://extranet.who.int/countryplanningcycles/sites/default/files/public_file_rep/RWA_Rwanda_Digital-Health-Strategy_2018-2023.Pdf)
- **LIS (laboratory)** — **National Reference Laboratory** runs a LabWare client-server LIS, upgraded to web-enabled, on the lab's local network; open-source **BLIS** adapted in satellite labs (esp. HIV testing); plan: synchronize local instances with a national LIS server. Source: [WHO extranet strategy PDF (pp. 35)](https://extranet.who.int/countryplanningcycles/sites/default/files/public_file_rep/RWA_Rwanda_Digital-Health-Strategy_2018-2023.Pdf)
- **eLMIS (logistics)** — **hosted application** (vendor-hosted, annual licence fees) rolled out to **all district pharmacies and public health facilities**; used for orders + stock visibility; interoperability with RBC/MPDD's SAGE financial system and (planned) DHIS2; HISP Rwanda showcased a DHIS2/mSupply eLMIS solution at the 2025 DHIS2 Annual Conference. Sources: [WHO extranet strategy PDF (pp. 46–48)](https://extranet.who.int/countryplanningcycles/sites/default/files/public_file_rep/RWA_Rwanda_Digital-Health-Strategy_2018-2023.Pdf), [HISP Rwanda DHIS2 page](https://hisprwanda.org/dhis2/)
- **Telemedicine** — first generation used DSL between referral hospitals (King Faisal Hospital etc.); evolving to 4G/video. Source: [WHO extranet strategy PDF (pp. 35–36)](https://extranet.who.int/countryplanningcycles/sites/default/files/public_file_rep/RWA_Rwanda_Digital-Health-Strategy_2018-2023.Pdf)

**Community tier (mobile / offline-first)**
- **RapidSMS → RapidPro (CHW reporting)** — national SMS platform running on **feature phones donated to CHWs** (45,000 phones, 98% network coverage); upgrade to RapidPro adds SMS/IVR/USSD/WhatsApp channels + Android "Surveyor" app for **offline data collection**. Sources: [WHO extranet strategy PDF (pp. 20, 44)](https://extranet.who.int/countryplanningcycles/sites/default/files/public_file_rep/RWA_Rwanda_Digital-Health-Strategy_2018-2023.Pdf), [PMI/CHI Rwanda digital community health profile (2021)](https://media.path.org/documents/Rwanda_PMIDCHI.pdf)
- **cEMR — community EMR on smartphones** — UNICEF-supported rollout: smartphones to 600 CHWs in 5 districts → 7,268 CHWs in 9 districts → government scaling to 17 districts (31,094 CHWs) and **nationwide (58,567 CHWs) by end-2026**. Source: [UNICEF Rwanda — From Piles of Registers to Digital Care](https://www.unicef.org/rwanda/stories/piles-registers-digital-care-community-health-workers-rwanda-adopt-digital-health-rec)
- **Empower CHWs / AKIISA (AI training platform)** — RBC + Expertise France (L'Initiative) AI-powered platform for 58,567 CHWs (e-learning, AI performance monitoring, gender-responsive matching); CIIC-HIN deploys the AKIISA platform. Source: [CIIC-HIN news 30 May 2025](https://ciichin.org/rwanda-launches-new-ai-powered-digital-platform-to-support-continuous-capacity-building-for-community-health-workers-and-advance-universal-health-coverage/)

**Cloud / private operators (external, not government-hosted)**
- **Babyl (telemedicine)** — UK-based Babylon Health operating in Rwanda since 2016 as "Babyl"; 10-year partnership with the Government of Rwanda; **built on AWS** (Babylon's own case-study material; Perplexity extraction), i.e. patient records in a UK-owned cloud, not the NDC; Babyl Rwanda was wound down after Babylon Health UK entered administration (Aug 2023, UK ops acquired by eMed). Sources: [MobiHealthNews — Babylon launches AI triage in Rwanda](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/emea/babylon-launches-ai-powered-triage-tool-rwanda), [Perplexity synthesis (Babyl/AWS — medium confidence)](out/.raw/pplx_deploy_4.json)
- **Zipline (drone logistics)** — physical distribution-centre network, not cloud: launched Oct 2016 with 15 drones delivering blood to 21 health facilities; centres in **Muhanga and Kayonza** (a third, Rusizi, added later); facilities order by **SMS/phone** to Zipline's dispatch; Dec 2022 national partnership targets ~2M deliveries and 200M autonomous km by 2029. Sources: [WHO extranet strategy PDF (p. 47)](https://extranet.who.int/countryplanningcycles/sites/default/files/public_file_rep/RWA_Rwanda_Digital-Health-Strategy_2018-2023.Pdf), [Emergency Live — Muhanga & Kayonza centres](https://www.emergency-live.com/marketplace/rwanda-blood-and-medical-supplies-to-hospitals-and-clinics-thanks-to-zipline-drones), [Zipline–Govt of Rwanda partnership (GlobeNewswire)](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2022/12/15/2574639/0/en/zipline-and-the-government-of-rwanda-announce-a-new-partnership-to-serve-the-entire-country-with-instant-logistics.html)
- **HR / payroll (IPPIS, iHRIS)** — IPPIS (Mifotra) is a **web-based government-wide HR/payroll system provided free to institutions**; iHRIS used by MoH since 2010; both national-tier but operated outside the health stack's NDC hosting discussion. Source: [WHO extranet strategy PDF (p. 54)](https://extranet.who.int/countryplanningcycles/sites/default/files/public_file_rep/RWA_Rwanda_Digital-Health-Strategy_2018-2023.Pdf)

## The government data-centre & cloud layer

- The **National Data Center (NDC)** — part of the NICI/Smart Rwanda programme led by RDB/MYICT — opened to the MoH in **2013**; RHMIS/DHIS2 instances were hosted there at **$5–7k/month**, and the 2018 plan flagged "**high cost of hosting in National Data Center that is well above international cloud hosting charges**". Sources: [allAfrica — RDB National Data Center (2009)](https://allafrica.com/stories/200905070364.html), [WHO extranet strategy PDF (pp. 20, 40, 58)](https://extranet.who.int/countryplanningcycles/sites/default/files/public_file_rep/RWA_Rwanda_Digital-Health-Strategy_2018-2023.Pdf)
- **RISA's digital-government guidance** explicitly weighs public cloud vs government data centres: "Public Cloud Operators utilize advanced security mechanisms… and as such can provide better security commitments than data hosted within the government entity data centers." Source: [RISA — Digital Government Platform Architecture guidelines](https://guidelines.risa.gov.rw/books/rwanda-digital-government-strategy-and-governance-Aj4/page/digital-government-platform-architecture/export/html)
- **Kigali data-centre market is growing** (for future cloud/colocation): Africa Data Centres building its first Kigali facility (2MW, ground broken 2023); PAIX Kigali inside Kigali Innovation City; TrAC Kigali (TransAfrica, described as Rwanda's Tier-III facilities); local cloud **Megabit Cloud** with two availability zones (Kigali AZ1, Huye AZ2) marketed at "government institutions" with Rwanda Data Protection Act compliance and in-country residency. Sources: [Cassava/Africa Data Centres](https://www.cassavatechnologies.com/africa-data-centres-to-build-its-first-data-centre-in-kigali/), [datacentermap Kigali](https://www.datacentermap.com/rwanda/kigali/), [Megabit Cloud Rwanda](https://megabitcloud.com/locations/rwanda)
- **In-country DC policy**: Rwanda's data-centre directives require facilities to be **"above Tier 3"** with N+1 redundancy (RURA licensing category "Data Center: Tier Three and above"). Source: [Perplexity synthesis of Rwanda DC policy (medium confidence)](out/.raw/pplx_deploy_5.json)

## Connectivity assumptions by tier

- **National**: fiber backbone + NDC hosting; MYICT/RDB extended fiber-optic and 4G to provide "last mile connectivity to health facilities". Source: [WHO extranet strategy PDF (p. 21)](https://extranet.who.int/countryplanningcycles/sites/default/files/public_file_rep/RWA_Rwanda_Digital-Health-Strategy_2018-2023.Pdf)
- **District hospitals**: "nearly all… connected to the national fiber optic internet connections" (2018 plan); 2015 survey: ~50% on fiber/ADSL broadband, ~80% of hospitals have servers capable of running hospital-management systems, ~25% have video-conferencing equipment. Sources: [WHO extranet strategy PDF (pp. 20, 57)](https://extranet.who.int/countryplanningcycles/sites/default/files/public_file_rep/RWA_Rwanda_Digital-Health-Strategy_2018-2023.Pdf)
- **Health centres**: ~80% connected via **wireless modems** (2015 survey); electricity from grid or solar; the eLMIS roll-out exposed that "some facilities – especially those with limited internet connections – do not systematically enter their stock movements on a daily basis". Sources: [WHO extranet strategy PDF (pp. 46, 57)](https://extranet.who.int/countryplanningcycles/sites/default/files/public_file_rep/RWA_Rwanda_Digital-Health-Strategy_2018-2023.Pdf), [PMI/CHI profile](https://media.path.org/documents/Rwanda_PMIDCHI.pdf)
- **Community**: ~95–98% cellular network coverage (PMI/CHI 2021: 95%; strategy 2018: 98%), with **offline-first Android apps** (RapidPro Surveyor; DHIS2 Android Capture App used for COVID testing) as the resilience mechanism for intermittent connectivity; 4G penetration surged from 2% to 30% in two years after Rwanda abandoned the single-wholesale-network model in 2022. Sources: [PMI/CHI profile](https://media.path.org/documents/Rwanda_PMIDCHI.pdf), [WHO extranet strategy PDF (p. 20)](https://extranet.who.int/countryplanningcycles/sites/default/files/public_file_rep/RWA_Rwanda_Digital-Health-Strategy_2018-2023.Pdf), [GSMA — single wholesale network reversal](https://www.gsma.com/connectivity-for-good/spectrum/abandoning-its-single-wholesale-network-unlocks-significant-connectivity-growth-for-rwanda/)

## Double-click 1 — DHIS2 / HMIS hosting arrangement

- **Where**: National Data Center since ~2013 (RHMIS instances), operated by RBC with HISP Rwanda as implementer; HISP project documents describe deployments "hosted on HISP servers" pending "RBC/MOH" server availability, i.e. a **transition from HISP-held to government-held infrastructure**. Sources: [docs.dhis2.org user story](https://docs.dhis2.org/en/topics/user-stories/rwanda-hmis-powered-by-dhis2.html), [WHO extranet strategy PDF (pp. 20, 40)](https://extranet.who.int/countryplanningcycles/sites/default/files/public_file_rep/RWA_Rwanda_Digital-Health-Strategy_2018-2023.Pdf)
- **Cost pressure**: NDC hosting for RHMIS instances ≈ **$5,000–7,000/month** (2018), flagged as above international cloud pricing — a standing argument for cloud migration. Source: [WHO extranet strategy PDF (p. 40)](https://extranet.who.int/countryplanningcycles/sites/default/files/public_file_rep/RWA_Rwanda_Digital-Health-Strategy_2018-2023.Pdf)
- **Multi-instance architecture**: multiple DHIS2 instances (HMIS, LMIS, TB, IDSR/EWARN, immunization/EPI, malaria, NCDs, COVID-19 surveillance/vaccine, BNA, VPD case-based surveillance) synchronized into a national Health Data Warehouse. Source: [HISP Rwanda DHIS2 page](https://hisprwanda.org/dhis2/), [WHO extranet strategy PDF (p. 40)](https://extranet.who.int/countryplanningcycles/sites/default/files/public_file_rep/RWA_Rwanda_Digital-Health-Strategy_2018-2023.Pdf)
- **Edge**: DHIS2 Android Capture App deployed on facility tablets/phones (COVID-19 testing end-to-end paperless). Source: [dhis2.org — COVID testing](https://dhis2.org/rwanda-covid-testing/)

## Double-click 2 — OpenMRS EMR facility deployment model

- **Current model (as of 2018)**: per-facility on-prem server ("installation of the EMR system on servers at every health facility"); HIV package in 362 facilities, billing package in 8 district hospitals; facility LANs and local servers standard at district hospitals. Source: [WHO extranet strategy PDF (pp. 20, 33–34)](https://extranet.who.int/countryplanningcycles/sites/default/files/public_file_rep/RWA_Rwanda_Digital-Health-Strategy_2018-2023.Pdf)
- **Planned trajectory**: mixed model — a small number of hospitals on a central server, rest local — then "**Migrate local EMRs onto the centralized server where possible**" and sync a national Shared Health Record via the HIE. Source: [WHO extranet strategy PDF (pp. 34–35)](https://extranet.who.int/countryplanningcycles/sites/default/files/public_file_rep/RWA_Rwanda_Digital-Health-Strategy_2018-2023.Pdf)
- **Community extension (cEMR)**: the CHW tier moves from feature-phone SMS (RapidSMS) to **smartphone-based community EMR** — 600 CHWs in 5 districts → 7,268 in 9 districts (UNICEF-supported) → national scale to 58,567 CHWs by end-2026. Source: [UNICEF Rwanda](https://www.unicef.org/rwanda/stories/piles-registers-digital-care-community-health-workers-rwanda-adopt-digital-health-rec), [PMI/CHI profile (2021 baseline: feature phones)](https://media.path.org/documents/Rwanda_PMIDCHI.pdf)

## Double-click 3 — NHIC infrastructure

- **Architecture (from NHIC's own site content)**: "NHIC runs on a unified, six-layer data pipeline… It starts at the source — hospitals, clinics, national programs, and community health workers across every district — and flows through automated ETL pipelines that clean, validate, and standardize it before it's **replicated across secure, redundant systems for resilience**. From there it lands in a **centralized data warehouse… tiered across hot and cold storage**… The result surfaces through interactive dashboards, open APIs, and downloadable reports." Source: [nhic.moh.gov.rw](https://nhic.moh.gov.rw/) (content extracted from the site's frontend bundle, `out/.raw/nhic_bundle.js`)
- **Concrete stack signals**: Apache **Superset** guest-token embedded dashboards; API at `https://api.nhic.moh.gov.rw/api`; datasets incl. National Health Facility Registry, District Health Performance Dashboard; portal states "All health data is anonymized and governed by Rwanda's data protection laws and NHIC's Privacy Policy". Source: [nhic.moh.gov.rw](https://nhic.moh.gov.rw/) (bundle)
- **Builders/partners**: TBI (Tony Blair Institute) provided technical expertise for the centralized data hub; NHIC frontend is hosted on a **kwikkoders.com subdomain** (platform builder/operator indicated, unconfirmed); launched 03 Apr 2025 at the Global AI Summit on Africa; physical HQ at the **RURA Building, Kiyovu (Péage), Nyarugenge District, Kigali** (MoH NHIC page: "Kigali, Rwanda"; July 2026 report specifies RURA HQ in Kiyovu). Sources: [TBI](https://institute.global/insights/public-services/how-rwanda-is-using-data-to-deliver-better-health-care), [MoH news](https://www.moh.gov.rw/news-detail/new-health-intelligence-center-to-drive-real-time-evidence-based-decisions), [Perplexity NHIC-host pass](out/.raw/pplx_deploy_7_nhic_host.json)
- **Gap**: NHIC's *infrastructure* hosting (NDC vs private cloud, provider, region, on-prem vs colocation) is **not publicly disclosed** — the "secure, redundant systems" claim and data-residency posture are architecture-level statements only; a World Bank digital-acceleration note frames Rwanda's broader government approach as "hybrid cloud-based solutions," but does not name NHIC's stack. Source: [Perplexity NHIC-host pass](out/.raw/pplx_deploy_7_nhic_host.json)

## Data-residency & DR/backup posture (deployment angle; controls detail is L4)

- **RHIE**: plan explicitly required "Ensure that the RHIE is **securely hosted and backed up regularly**" (2018). Source: [WHO extranet strategy PDF (p. 32)](https://extranet.who.int/countryplanningcycles/sites/default/files/public_file_rep/RWA_Rwanda_Digital-Health-Strategy_2018-2023.Pdf)
- **NHIC**: replication across redundant systems + hot/cold warehouse tiering (resilience + archival). Source: [nhic.moh.gov.rw](https://nhic.moh.gov.rw/)
- **NDC-hosted systems**: backup/DR/SSL "developed and enforced" was a 2018 strategic commitment; electricity/lightning/equipment-replacement risks documented. Source: [WHO extranet strategy PDF (pp. 57–60)](https://extranet.who.int/countryplanningcycles/sites/default/files/public_file_rep/RWA_Rwanda_Digital-Health-Strategy_2018-2023.Pdf)
- **Cross-border flows**: Babyl's patient records sat on Babylon's (UK) AWS cloud — the one documented case of PHI leaving Rwanda's national infrastructure; the 2018 strategy predates Law 058/2021, which now governs such transfers (details in L7). Sources: [Perplexity synthesis (medium confidence)](out/.raw/pplx_deploy_4.json), [MobiHealthNews](https://www.mobihealthnews.com/news/emea/babylon-launches-ai-powered-triage-tool-rwanda)
- **In-country alternatives**: Megabit Cloud (Kigali+Huye) markets Rwandan data residency + DP-Act compliance; TrAC and PAIX offer Tier-III colocation; NDC remains the government default. Sources: [Megabit Cloud](https://megabitcloud.com/locations/rwanda), [datacentermap](https://www.datacentermap.com/rwanda/kigali/)

## Gaps + confidence

- **High confidence**: NDC as the national hosting anchor since 2013; DHIS2/HMIS multi-instance + data-warehouse architecture; EMR on-prem facility-server model with a centralized migration plan; NHIC's six-layer pipeline/ETL/warehouse/Superset stack (from its own site bundle) + physical HQ at RURA Building, Kiyovu, Kigali; Zipline centre network + SMS ordering; CHW tier transition feature-phone → smartphone/cEMR; connectivity stats (2015 survey, 95–98% coverage, GSMA 4G growth); **no verified move of the national health stack to AWS/Azure/GCP as of 2024–26** — the live HMIS portal remains under the MoH domain and the best-sourced position remains NDC/ministry-controlled hosting (Perplexity deploy_6 pass).
- **Medium confidence**: exact *current* production hosting of DHIS2 (HISP-server vs RBC/MoH server transition is documented but the 2023–26 endpoint is not); eLMIS hosting platform today (2018: hosted vendor app with licence fees; 2025: HISP/DHIS2-mSupply work shown — product/owner unresolved); Babyl's AWS region and Rwanda data-residency specifics (Babylon case-study material, not an official Rwanda-facing disclosure); "Tier 3+" DC policy details (Perplexity-synthesized); NHIC implementer (kwikkoders indicated via subdomain, unconfirmed).
- **Low/unknown**: NHIC application hosting provider and region (RURA HQ confirms physical presence, not infrastructure); RHIE production status post-2018; NIB-specific data-centre facility and current government-cloud tenant list; whether any health workloads sit in Kigali's private DCs (PAIX/TrAC/Africa Data Centres) or on Megabit Cloud today; existence of any MINICT/NCSA health-specific cloud adoption or data-residency policy (not found in public sources).
