# Rwanda MoH Digital Health — Data-Protection Approach (DP)

Lane: `dp_approach` · Focus: how the **deployed** systems actually handle data protection in practice — consent capture, de-identification, access control, data residency, audit logging, encryption at-rest / in-transit — with a double-click on the RHIE/OpenHIE exchange layer, the OpenMRS national EMR, and the DHIS2 HMIS.

---

## Overview

Rwanda is one of the few countries in the region with a **dedicated, in-force data-protection law that directly governs health data**: Law n° 058/2021 of 13/10/2021 (effective 15 Oct 2021), supervised by the **National Cyber Security Authority (NCSA)**, with health-sector guidance layered on top through the Ministry of Health. The legal default is **data residency** — personal data must be stored in Rwanda unless the NCSA certifies otherwise — and sensitive data (explicitly including medical and health records) must be protected with measures such as **separate storage, tokenisation, pseudonymisation, or encryption**. In practice, the deployed MoH stack (OpenMRS national EMR, DHIS2 HMIS, the RHIE/OpenHIE health-information-exchange, eLMIS, NIDA-linked identity) protects data "primarily through policy, access control, and infrastructure controls" rather than through documented, per-system patient-facing consent workflows. Public deployment documentation is **much stronger on confidentiality, authentication, RBAC, encryption, and residency than on consent capture or systematic de-identification**, and the clearest documented de-identification workflow sits in **secondary HIV research reuse of OpenMRS data (IeDEA)** rather than in routine clinical operations. Confidence is **high** on the legal framework, **medium** on the exchange-layer (RHIE) architecture and secondary-use de-identification, and **low-to-medium** on at-rest encryption specifics and audit-log implementation for the national production systems — several Rwanda-specific nuances are documented as *intended* safeguards rather than observed audit-trail production detail.

## Discovered inventory (what actually handles DP in the deployed stack)

- **Law n° 058/2021 of 13/10/2021 (Protection of Personal Data and Privacy)** — the binding DP baseline for every health system: NCSA supervisory authority; opt-in consent; sensitive data includes health/medical records; storage in Rwanda required unless NCSA-certified; 48h breach notification; fines RWF 2–5M or 1% turnover (admin) and criminal offences for e.g. re-identifying de-identified data. Source: https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?c=RW&t=law
- **NCSA (National Cyber Security Authority)** — regulator for registration of controllers/processors, breach notification, cross-border transfer authorisation, and inspections; health sector is primarily regulated by MoH with DP compliance layered via NCSA rules and a dedicated "Guidance on Personal Data Protection in the Health Sector". Sources: https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?c=RW&t=law ; Perplexity sonar-pro synthesis (out/.raw/pplx_dp_law.json) citing the health-sector guidance.
- **DHIS2-based Rwanda HMIS (R-HMIS)** — the national reporting system, operational countrywide since February 2012, run by MoH at hmis.moh.gov.rw; carries aggregated + programmatic health data under confidentiality rules; DP posture comes from DHIS2 platform security (RBAC, audit, TLS) plus national ICT policy. Sources: https://docs.dhis2.org/es_419/topics/user-stories/rwanda-hmis-powered-by-dhis2.html ; https://hmis.moh.gov.rw/
- **OpenMRS national EMR** — countrywide OpenMRS installation since 2006, per-facility Ubuntu/LAN servers maintained by the Rwandan government; the documented de-identification/transfer control is the IeDEA secondary-use pipeline (custom C# identifier-stripping before upload to a US coordinating center). Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6918068/ ; https://github.com/PIH/openmrs-module-imbemr (IMB Rwanda OpenMRS EMR)
- **RHIE / RHIES (Rwanda Health Information Exchange System)** — OpenHIE-based exchange: OpenHIM interoperability layer + mediators (OpenMRS↔DHIS2 e-tracker, OpenMRS↔NIDA, LabWare↔OpenMRS LIS), Client Registry (HAPI FHIR), Facility Registry (JSON/MongoDB); 6 OpenMRS instances on the RHIES server with plans for a Shared Health Record and 123 cloud OpenMRS instances. Sources: https://ohie.org/impact-stories/creating-a-health-information-exchange-system-in-rwanda ; https://exchange.dial.global/projects/rwanda-health-information-exchange-system-rhies ; https://wiki.ohie.org/pages/viewpage.action?pageId=10486056 ; https://www.researchgate.net/publication/262912516_An_Architecture_and_Reference_Implementation_of_an_Open_Health_Information_Mediator_Enabling_Interoperability_in_the_Rwandan_Health_Information_Exchange
- **NIDA (National Identification Agency) integration** — RHIES pulls citizen demographics from the national ID database into OpenMRS; identity-verification layer that makes the patient identity graph a national asset and a residency/access-control touchpoint. Source: https://ohie.org/impact-stories/creating-a-health-information-exchange-system-in-rwanda
- **eLMIS (electronic Logistics Management Information System) / LabWare LIS / RapidSMS** — supply-chain, lab, and community (SMS) systems connected around the exchange layer; DP practice follows national ICT security policy (encryption for sensitive data, VPN for remote access) rather than system-specific public DP docs. Sources: https://extranet.who.int/countryplanningcycles/sites/default/files/public_file_rep/RWA_Rwanda_Digital-Health-Strategy_2018-2023.Pdf ; Perplexity synthesis (out/.raw/pplx_dp_systems.json).
- **National Digital Health Strategic Plan 2018–2023** — policy anchor: adopted health-sector data-sharing policy, ICT security/backup/disaster-recovery/SSL SOPs, and confidentiality classification; notes the sharing policy originally covered only access and was being broadened. Source: https://extranet.who.int/countryplanningcycles/sites/default/files/public_file_rep/RWA_Rwanda_Digital-Health-Strategy_2018-2023.Pdf

## Double-click 1 — RHIE / OpenHIE exchange layer (the DP choke-point)

The RHIE is the single most important DP control surface because it is where identifiable patient data crosses system boundaries (OpenMRS, NIDA, DHIS2, LabWare) through one interoperability layer.

- **Architecture & data flow.** OpenHIM is the interoperability/mediator layer; mediators move data OpenMRS→DHIS2 (HIV CBS e-tracker), OpenMRS↔NIDA (demographic pull), LabWare→OpenMRS (LIS results); Client Registry is HAPI FHIR, Facility Registry JSON/MongoDB; registries are pushed back into the EMRs to keep identities accurate. Every new patient in OpenMRS enters the client-registry workflow. Source: https://ohie.org/impact-stories/creating-a-health-information-exchange-system-in-rwanda ; https://www.researchgate.net/publication/262912516_An_Architecture_and_Reference_Implementation_of_an_Open_Health_Information_Mediator_Enabling_Interoperability_in_the_Rwandan_Health_Information_Exchange
- **Consent model (exchange layer).** A Rwanda HIE source describes a privacy model with **a consent model that blocks access to a patient's record at the patient's request** plus **element-level sensitivity controls** (sensitive elements can be blocked selectively) — i.e., consent-enforced *deny* at the record and element level, rather than a documented capture/revocation workflow. No public Rwanda material details how consent is captured or propagated across systems. Source: Perplexity synthesis citing Rwanda HIE privacy/security literature (out/.raw/pplx_dc_openmrs_rhie.json) — no direct public URL for the workflow itself; flagged as a gap.
- **Access control & residency.** RHIE reporting explicitly states personal data must be **stored in Rwanda** unless the controller/processor holds NCSA authorisation to store abroad (echoing Law 058/2021); data-controller/processor roles and "robust protections" are invoked, but the exact RBAC design of the exchange is not publicly specified. Sources: Perplexity synthesis (out/.raw/pplx_dp_systems.json) ; law at https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?c=RW&t=law
- **Encryption & audit.** The national ICT security policy requires encryption for sensitive data and VPN tunnels for remote connections; RHIE is expected to operate inside that framework. A Rwanda-specific audit-log implementation for the exchange layer was **not** found in public docs. Source: Perplexity synthesis (out/.raw/pplx_dp_systems.json).
- **Note:** The OpenHIE architecture specification (the design RHIE follows) is public and specifies security services (authentication, authorization, audit logging) at the architecture level — useful for "intended design" but not proof of Rwanda production configuration. Source: https://ohie.org/wp-content/uploads/2020/12/OpenHIE-Specification-Release-3.0.pdf

## Double-click 2 — OpenMRS national EMR (consent, de-identification, RBAC)

- **Deployment model.** Countrywide since 2006; each clinic runs an **Ubuntu Linux server with OpenMRS on a local LAN** to keep the EMR available without internet; infrastructure maintained by the Government of Rwanda, which supports secondary use for research. Source: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6918068/
- **De-identification in practice (strongest documented control).** The IeDEA HIV-research pipeline: a local data manager visits each clinic bi-annually, **extracts patient data from the clinic's OpenMRS database and runs a custom C# program that removes patient identifiers**; with **Rwandan government and ethics-board permission**, de-identified copies are **securely uploaded to the Central Africa IeDEA coordinating center in the US** for data-quality checks and regional merging. This is best characterised as irreversible **de-identification**, not reversible pseudonymisation (no tokenisation/re-identification key is described). An earlier approach using an OpenMRS module generating **encrypted data export files** was abandoned in favour of this local de-identification model. Sources: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6918068/ (Muhoza et al., Stud Health Technol Inform 2019; doi:10.3233/SHTI190620)
- **Consent capture.** No public Rwanda deployment doc describes a patient-facing consent workflow inside OpenMRS for routine clinical use; consent language appears in the HIE privacy discussion rather than the clinic workflow. Source: Perplexity synthesis (out/.raw/pplx_dc_openmrs_rhie.json).
- **RBAC & authentication.** A Rwanda OpenMRS/PIH thesis describes "user authentication" and "privilege-based access: user roles and permission system" as core security features, with SSL cited for confidentiality; the Rwanda EMR implementation is open-sourced (PIH `openmrs-module-imbemr`), where OpenMRS's role/privilege model (`View Patients`, `Edit Patients`, etc.) is visible. Sources: https://github.com/PIH/openmrs-module-imbemr ; https://guide.openmrs.org/administering-openmrs/user-management-and-access-control/ ; Perplexity synthesis (out/.raw/pplx_dc_openmrs_rhie.json)
- **Encryption at rest / in transit.** SSL/TLS is cited for the Rwanda deployment; **server-side at-rest encryption for the national OpenMRS production environment is not publicly documented** (and an OpenMRS security assessment notes some files "appear to be" not encrypted in the assessed context). Mobile/Android OpenMRS guidance references AES-256 for the local DB + SSL transport, but that is not Rwanda-deployment-specific. Sources: Perplexity synthesis (out/.raw/pplx_dc_openmrs_rhie.json) — flagged as a gap.
- **Audit logging.** Authentication-attempt logging is part of the OpenMRS security model under review, but a Rwanda-publicly-documented audit-trail architecture (who read which record, immutable logs, retention) was **not** found. Source: Perplexity synthesis (out/.raw/pplx_dc_openmrs_rhie.json).

## Double-click 3 — DHIS2-based Rwanda HMIS (aggregate surveillance vs individual data)

- **Deployment.** The web-based R-HMIS has been operational countrywide since February 2012 and is "recognised as a trailblazer in its category"; it is the national repository for facility-reported health statistics (MoH-run portal). Sources: https://docs.dhis2.org/es_419/topics/user-stories/rwanda-hmis-powered-by-dhis2.html ; https://hmis.moh.gov.rw/
- **DP posture.** HMIS is treated as a health-sector reporting system under confidentiality/personal-data rules; Rwanda-specific public deployment docs do **not** describe patient-consent capture or systematic de-identification inside DHIS2 — the platform's privacy stance is aggregate-level reporting, with individual (Tracker) data used for program surveillance (e.g., HIV CBS pushed from OpenMRS via RHIE). Sources: https://docs.dhis2.org/es_419/topics/user-stories/rwanda-hmis-powered-by-dhis2.html ; https://ohie.org/impact-stories/creating-a-health-information-exchange-system-in-rwanda ; Perplexity synthesis (out/.raw/pplx_dp_systems.json)
- **Platform-level DP capabilities (the controls Rwanda inherits).** DHIS2 documentation defines the security model a national instance can deploy: legal/regulatory + human/organisational context analysis; organisational measures; system-configuration measures; and technical measures — plus user roles/groups and authorities, sharing, an **audit** concept/API, TLS guidance, and Android "data security and privacy" guidance. Sources: https://docs.dhis2.org/en/implement/implementing-dhis2/security-considerations.html ; https://docs.dhis2.org/en/manage/concepts/audit.html ; https://docs.dhis2.org/en/implement/maintenance-and-use/users-and-user-roles.html
- **Residency.** Same national rule: personal data stored in Rwanda unless NCSA authorises otherwise; RHIE reporting repeats this for exchange participants. Sources: https://www.dlapiperdataprotection.com/?c=RW&t=law ; Perplexity synthesis (out/.raw/pplx_dp_systems.json)

## Control-by-control summary (across the deployed stack)

| Control | Law / policy baseline | Documented in practice | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|
| Consent capture | Opt-in consent; child <16 parental consent; public-health and preventive-medicine lawful bases (Law 058/2021) | HIE-level "block access at patient request" + element-level sensitivity; **no public per-system capture workflow** | High (law) / Low (practice) |
| De-identification | Required as an option for sensitive data (tokenisation, pseudonymisation, encryption; art. 11) | **IeDEA OpenMRS pipeline**: custom C# identifier-stripping before US upload (research use only) | Medium |
| Access control | Controllers/processors must register; RBAC mandated by DHIS2/OpenMRS platform models | OpenMRS roles/privileges (PIH IMB EMR); DHIS2 user roles/groups; NIDA-linked identity | Medium-High |
| Data residency | **Storage in Rwanda unless NCSA-certified** (art. 50); cross-border transfers conditional (art. 48–49) | Explicitly repeated in RHIE reporting; IeDEA US transfers occur with government + ethics permission | High |
| Audit logging | Breach notification 48h to NCSA (art. 43); security-verification duty (art. 47) | Platform-level audit APIs exist (DHIS2, OpenHIE spec); **no Rwanda production audit-trail doc found** | Low-Medium |
| Encryption at rest / in transit | Encryption named as a sensitive-data safeguard (art. 11); MoH ICT policy: encryption + VPN | SSL cited for OpenMRS Rwanda; DHIS2 TLS guidance; **at-rest encryption for national prod not documented** | Low-Medium |

## Gaps + confidence

- **What I could not verify (gaps):** (1) a public, per-system **patient consent capture/revocation workflow** in OpenMRS, DHIS2, or RHIE for routine clinical care — only policy-level requirements and an HIE-level consent-deny model surfaced; (2) **at-rest encryption** (disk/DB-level) for the national OpenMRS, RHIE, or DHIS2 production servers — only SSL/VPN in transit and laptop-encryption policy; (3) **production audit-log specifics** (who-read-which-record, immutable logs, retention) for any named national system; (4) current **cloud-hosting residency details** — the RHIES plan to deploy 123 OpenMRS instances "on the cloud" (per the 2021 OpenHIE impact story) raises an unverified residency question under art. 50; (5) NCSA registration status of MoH systems.
- **Engine degradation:** Perplexity `sonar-pro` results returned bracket citations without URL mappings, so several Perplexity-derived claims are anchored to the synthesis dumps (out/.raw/pplx_*.json) and cross-checked against the primary URLs listed above; DIAL project page and ResearchGate returned empty/truncated bodies on direct fetch (title/metadata only). No engine call failed outright.
- **Confidence:** Law/policy framework — **high** (DLA Piper country guide + Perplexity; cross-checked against each other). RHIE architecture + NIDA/OpenHIM integration — **medium-high** (OpenHIE impact story, DIAL, OpenHIE wiki, ResearchGate). IeDEA de-identification — **high for that specific pipeline** (peer-reviewed PMC paper), but it is research secondary-use, not routine clinical DP. DHIS2 HMIS — **medium** (platform docs + user story; no Rwanda-specific security audit). Consent capture, at-rest encryption, audit logs in national production — **low** (no public deployment evidence; say so rather than invent).
